What was corrected

The draft’s optimism was replaced with traceable assumptions.

01

Revenue was rebuilt

2025 salesperson and product performance was adjusted for 2026 marketing support, actual staff availability and the registered-program floor.

02

Departures were respected

Anna’s employee sales stop after February. Ella produces no revenue during May onboarding, then ramps through June and July.

03

Every euro was costed

All forecast revenue uses the 2025 monthly direct-cost rate, including the supplier-rate increase from July.

04

Revenue follows delivery

Proposals and cash timing do not create P&L revenue. Income is recognized in the expected delivery month.

05

Opex timing was repaired

Insurance is accrued monthly, payroll follows the HR plan and studio depreciation starts when the asset enters service in May.

06

Capacity was made visible

Trainer days and open seats are calculated explicitly, including the need for extra September and October program dates.

Part 2

Three decisions create the recovery.

Each decision is explicit, timed and separately measurable.

01

Client continuity

Retain Anna’s client relationships as a contractor.

Agree terms in March at 10% commission on attributable delivered revenue. Budget 60% of her 2025 same-month revenue from May through December.

02

Demand generation

Move the corporate campaign earlier.

Add €1,500 per month from March to June and enforce weekly lead ownership. Five modeled wins are delivered from August to November.

03

Delivery economics

Use internal instructor capacity first.

From May, assign the internal senior instructor to 50% of forecast delivery days, capped by monthly capacity; retain external cover for the balance.
